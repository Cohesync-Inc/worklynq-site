Modules

Worklynq is one platform made of modules. You switch on the ones that fit your business and leave out the rest — a restaurant doesn’t need release planning, and a software team doesn’t need store checklists. The modules are grouped into four services. Everything shares the same people, permissions, and AI, so work, knowledge, and customers stay connected.

Turning modules on and off. Your System Admin picks modules during setup and can change them anytime in the Admin console. Some modules need another one (Team Board needs Tickets, for example). Turning a module off only hides it — nothing is deleted, and it comes back as you left it.

Service

Workspace

Plan it. Run it. Deliver it.

Workspace is where the day-to-day work lives. Every request, task, checklist, job, and release is a ticket, organized around how your business actually runs — by store, by project, or by product — so everyone can see what's happening and what's next.

Tickets

Every piece of work, tracked from start to done.

Tickets hold everything your team works on: tasks, requests, issues, orders, inspections, bugs. Each ticket type has its own workflow — a starting stage, up to five stages in the middle, and a finished stage — and can run in a set order or move freely. Types can sit under one another, like a Story under an Epic or a Punch Item under a Milestone.

  • Ticket types with their own stages and colors
  • Parent and child tickets (Epic → Story, Milestone → Punch Item)
  • Assignees, priorities, due dates, comments, and attachments
  • Automations on each stage: create follow-up tickets, update fields, or hand the ticket to an AI agent
  • A library of 35 ready-made ticket types by industry

Operations Coming in the beta

Run every store, every day.

For businesses that never “finish” a project — a café, a shop, a hotel, a kitchen. Each of your stores or sites gets its own space with a day-by-day schedule, recurring checklists that create themselves, and its own dashboard. Routine work is marked so it never gets mixed up with one-off requests.

  • One space per store, site, or kitchen
  • Daily view of everything scheduled or due, by person
  • Recurring checklists and tickets (daily, weekly, monthly, set times) with a shared color and marker
  • A dashboard for each store

Needs: Tickets

Team Board

See the work. Keep everyone moving.

A shared board for planning and running work: keep a backlog, move tickets across the board as they progress, and plan in sprints if your team works that way.

  • Backlog planning with drag and drop
  • Board view by stage
  • Sprints
  • Filter by person, type, or priority

Needs: Tickets

Roadmaps

Turn plans into a timeline.

Lay out what's coming over the next weeks and months and connect it to the tickets that make it happen — a product roadmap, a construction schedule, or next season's store openings.

  • Initiatives on a timeline
  • Linked to tickets and releases
  • Progress at a glance

Needs: Tickets

Product Releases

Ship with confidence.

Plan each release, see what's in it and what's still blocking it, and keep release notes next to the work. Mostly used by software teams.

  • Release planning and scope
  • Readiness tracking
  • Release notes

Needs: Tickets

Dashboards

Your business at a glance.

Build dashboards from widgets — counts and KPIs, tickets by any field, today's schedule, overdue work, checklist completion, trends over time — and share them with your team. Make one for the whole company, one per team, or one per store. Reports live here too.

  • Dashboards built from widgets
  • Shared with your team, on any device
  • One per store, team, or product
  • Reports

Service

Knowledge Center

Your business's know-how, in one trusted place.

Knowledge Center keeps the things your team needs to know — how you open the store, how you run a job, how the product works — written down, approved, and easy to find. It's also what Worklynq's AI reads, so its answers are based on how your business actually works.

Spaces

Write it down once. Share it with the right people.

Personal and team spaces for documents, notes, and procedures. Write in a rich editor, start from templates, send important documents for approval, and archive what's out of date without losing it.

  • Personal and team spaces
  • Rich editor and templates
  • Approvals before a document is final
  • Version history
  • Archive that stays searchable

Knowledge Library

The company handbook that's always current.

Company-wide knowledge that belongs to everyone: policies, procedures, guides, playbooks, and standards. People find answers without asking around, and AI agents use the Library as trusted context.

  • Policies and procedures (SOPs)
  • Guides, playbooks, and standards
  • Searchable by everyone
  • Trusted context for AI

Needs: Spaces

Service

AI Hub

AI that works alongside your team.

AI Hub brings AI into the work instead of bolting a chatbot onto it. Ask questions across your business, hand tickets to AI agents, and shape how AI behaves — while people stay in charge of the decisions.

AI Search

Ask a question. Get an answer, with sources.

Ask in plain language — “What's still open at the downtown store?” or “What did we decide about the supplier change?” — and get an answer drawn from your tickets, documents, and connected tools, with links back to where it came from. You only see answers from what you're allowed to see.

  • Plain-language questions
  • Answers with links to the source
  • Respects everyone's permissions
  • Across tickets, documents, and connected tools

AI Agents

Hand the ticket to an AI teammate.

Hand a ticket to Claude or Cursor and they do the work: an agent reads the ticket, works in the product's code, and comes back with a pull request — all shown on the ticket. Start an agent from the ticket, when you create it, or automatically when a ticket reaches a certain stage.

  • Claude and Cursor agents
  • Start from a ticket, on create, or by automation
  • Status, summary, changes, and pull request on the ticket
  • Turn agents on or off per ticket type

Needs: Tickets

AI Context

Teach the AI how your business works.

Choose the documents, rules, and knowledge your AI should rely on, so its answers and actions fit your business instead of the internet's average.

  • Curated knowledge collections
  • Business rules and terminology
  • Controlled, trusted sources

Workflows

Multi-step work, with AI doing the legwork.

Chain steps together — gather information, draft, check, act — with a person approving the steps that matter.

  • Multi-step AI workflows
  • Human approval where it matters
  • Triggered by events or on demand

Assets

Build it once. Reuse it everywhere.

Keep reusable prompts, instructions, and context in one place so every agent and workflow behaves consistently.

  • Reusable prompts and instructions
  • Shared across agents and workflows
  • One place to update

Playground

Try it before your team relies on it.

Test models, prompts, and context side by side, see what the AI used and what it cost, and only roll out what works.

  • Compare models and prompts
  • See the context the AI used
  • Cost and speed at a glance
