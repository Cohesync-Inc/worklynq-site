Modules

Worklynq is one platform made of modules. You switch on the ones that fit your business and leave out the rest — a restaurant doesn’t need release planning, and a software team doesn’t need store checklists. This page groups the modules into four services to make them easier to browse. In the app there’s just one sidebar: it lists the modules your company has turned on, in the order your System Admin chooses, and leaves out anything your role can’t open. Everything shares the same people, permissions, and AI, so work, knowledge, and customers stay connected.

Turning modules on and off. Your System Admin picks modules during setup and can change them anytime in Admin console → Work Configuration → Modules. There they can drag modules into the order the sidebar should show them, see a live preview of what the team sees, or reset to the recommended set for your business type. Some modules need another one (Team Board needs Tickets, for example) — turning one on turns on what it needs, and turning one off turns off what depends on it. Turning a module off only hides it — nothing is deleted, and it comes back as you left it.

Service

Workspace

Plan it. Run it. Deliver it.

Workspace is where the day-to-day work lives. Every request, task, checklist, job, and release is a ticket, organized around how your business actually runs — by store, by project, or by product — so everyone can see what's happening and what's next.

Tickets

Every piece of work, tracked from start to done.

Tickets hold everything your team works on: tasks, requests, issues, orders, inspections, bugs. Each ticket type has its own workflow — a starting stage, up to five stages in the middle, and up to four end stages — and can run in a set order or move freely. Every end stage is marked as a success, a failure, or cancelled, so work can finish more than one way: a Change Order can end Approved, Rejected, or Withdrawn. The workflow editor draws several end stages as branches. Types can sit under one another, like a Story under an Epic or a Punch Item under a Milestone.

  • Ticket types with their own stages and colors
  • Several endings per type — success, failure, or cancelled
  • Parent and child tickets (Epic → Story, Milestone → Punch Item)
  • Assignees, priorities, due dates, comments, and attachments
  • Automations on each stage: create follow-up tickets, update fields, or hand the ticket to an AI agent
  • A library of 35 ready-made ticket types by industry

Operations Coming in the beta

Run every store, every day.

For businesses that never “finish” a project — a café, a shop, a hotel, a kitchen. Each of your stores or sites gets its own space with a day-by-day schedule, recurring checklists that create themselves, and its own dashboard. Routine work is marked so it never gets mixed up with one-off requests.

  • One space per store, site, or kitchen
  • Daily view of everything scheduled or due, by person
  • Recurring checklists and tickets (daily, weekly, monthly, set times) with a shared color and marker
  • A dashboard for each store

Needs: Tickets

Approvals

Get a yes or no, on the record.

For requests that need sign-off before they go ahead — documents, purchases, access, time off. Each type of request has its own stages, and every request ends approved, rejected, or withdrawn. Approved document approvals mark Knowledge Center pages as Verified. Optional: it’s off until your System Admin turns it on, whatever your business type.

  • Document, access, purchase, and time-off types ready to use
  • Add your own types, each with its own stages
  • Approved, rejected, and withdrawn endings
  • How Approvals works

Team Board

See the work. Keep everyone moving.

A shared board for planning and running work: keep a backlog, move tickets across the board as they progress, and plan in sprints if your team works that way.

  • Backlog planning with drag and drop
  • Board view by stage
  • Sprints
  • Filter by person, type, or priority

Needs: Tickets

Product Releases

Ship with confidence.

Plan each release, see what's in it and what's still blocking it, and keep release notes next to the work. Mostly used by software teams.

  • Release planning and scope

Dashboards & Reports

Your business at a glance — and in your inbox.

Build dashboards and reports from widgets — counts and KPIs, tickets by any field, today’s schedule, overdue work, completion, and trends — plus data from your connected tools, like Square sales and QuickBooks profit and loss. Keep them private, share them with a team, or with everyone, and pin one to a store, project, or product. Describe the dashboard you want and AI builds it, with a live preview before anything is saved; click Explain and AI tells you what the numbers say — using Worklynq’s own AI, or a Claude or OpenAI model your company has set up. Reports can be emailed on a schedule, with an AI summary, to each person as they see it.

  • Dashboards and reports built from widgets
  • Data from Square, QuickBooks, and more
  • Shared with only you, a team, or everyone
  • One per store, project, or product
  • AI builds and explains them — or ask your own AI app
  • Scheduled reports with an AI summary

Service

Knowledge Center

Your business's know-how, in one trusted place.

Knowledge Center keeps the things your team needs to know — how you open the store, how you run a job, how the product works — written down, approved, and easy to find. It's also what Worklynq's AI reads, so its answers are based on how your business actually works.

Spaces

Write it down once. Share it with the right people.

Your own My Space plus shared spaces for the company, all in one place — pick a space at the top of the page tree, with My Space first. Write documents, notes, and procedures in a rich editor, start from templates, send important documents for approval (with the Approvals module on), and archive what's out of date without losing it.

  • My Space and shared spaces, with one space picker
  • Rich editor and templates
  • Verified pages, with Approvals
  • Version history
  • Archive that stays searchable
  • Pages shared with you stay private to the people they’re shared with

Knowledge Library

The company handbook that's always current.

Company-wide knowledge that belongs to everyone: policies, procedures, guides, playbooks, and standards. People find answers without asking around, and AI agents use the Library as trusted context.

  • Policies and procedures (SOPs)
  • Guides, playbooks, and standards
  • Searchable by everyone
  • Trusted context for AI

Needs: Spaces

AI Hub

AI that works alongside your team.

AI Hub brings AI into the work instead of bolting a chatbot onto it. Ask questions across your business, hand tickets to AI agents, and shape how AI behaves — while people stay in charge of the decisions.

AI Search

Ask a question. Get an answer, with sources.

Ask in plain language — “What's still open at the downtown store?” or “What did we decide about the supplier change?” — and get an answer drawn from your tickets, documents, and connected tools, with links back to where it came from. You only see answers from what you're allowed to see: AI Search checks your roles before it searches each source, never shows other people’s personal pages, and only searches your own Gmail if you connected it.

  • Plain-language questions
  • Answers with links to the source
  • Respects everyone's permissions
  • Across tickets, documents, and connected tools

AI Agents

Hand the ticket to an AI teammate.

Hand a ticket to Claude or Cursor and they do the work: an agent reads the ticket, works in the product's code, and comes back with a pull request — all shown on the ticket. Start an agent from the ticket, when you create it, or automatically when a ticket reaches a certain stage.

  • Claude and Cursor agents
  • Start from a ticket, on create, or by automation
  • Status, summary, changes, and pull request on the ticket
  • Turn agents on or off per ticket type
  • Use Worklynq from your own Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor

Needs: Tickets

Customer Experience

Every customer, every conversation, in one place.

Taking care of customers looks the same in every business. Customer Experience brings it together — from the first question to the repeat order — for every company type. Order Tracking is available now; the rest rolls out through the beta.

Order Tracking

Nobody has to ask twice.

Follow every customer order from new to completed on a board with a column per stage — New, Confirmed, In progress, Ready, On the way. Add items and prices, choose pickup, delivery, shipping, or in store, set a due time, and assign it to someone. Overdue orders are outlined in red, and every order keeps a history of who did what and when. On from the start for Operations and Software businesses.

  • Board and list, with drag and drop
  • Pickup, delivery, shipping, or in store
  • Due times, with overdue orders flagged
  • Order history and customer links
  • How Order Tracking works
