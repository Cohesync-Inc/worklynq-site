Modules

Worklynq is built from modules. Your company turns on the ones that fit the way it works and leaves out the rest — a café doesn’t see release planning, and a software team doesn’t see store schedules. The modules you turn on sit together in one sidebar, in the order your System Admin chooses. Everything shares the same people, permissions, and AI, so work, knowledge, and customers stay connected.

Prefer to watch? This short video shows System Admins how to turn modules on and off. More are on Video tutorials.

How modules work

  • Your business type sets the starting point. When you sign up, you say what kind of company you are — Operations, Projects, or Software — and Worklynq turns on the modules that fit. See Modules by business type.
  • One sidebar, in your order. The modules that are on appear in the sidebar in the order your System Admin sets. Each person only sees the modules their role lets them open. Your company’s first module is where Worklynq opens.
  • Some modules need another one. Turning a module on also turns on what it needs, and turning one off also turns off what depends on it. The table below lists them.
  • Off means hidden, never deleted. A module that’s off disappears for everyone, including its pages if someone follows an old link. Its data is kept, and it comes back as you left it when you turn it on again.
  • Every module is included. You don’t pay extra to turn one on.

Change your modules

Only the System Admin can change modules. Everyone else can see the page but not change it.

  1. Choose System Admin in the account menu at the bottom of the sidebar — the Admin console opens straight away — then open Work Configuration → Modules.
  2. Turn modules on or off. The page lists the modules that are on, in sidebar order, and below them the ones that are off. A note tells you when turning one on or off also changed another.
  3. Drag a row, or use its arrows, to change where it sits in the sidebar.
  4. Check What your team sees — a live preview of the sidebar that updates as you edit.
  5. Click Save changes. The sidebar changes for everyone in your company straight away.

Changed too much? Reset to recommended puts back the starting set for your business type — or for another type you pick — so you can save it or adjust it first. At least one module always stays on.

Service

Workspace

Plan it. Run it. Deliver it.

Workspace is where the day-to-day work lives. Every request, task, checklist, job, and release is a ticket, organized around how your business actually runs — by store, by project, or by product — so everyone can see what's happening and what's next.

Home

Where everyone starts.

Home shows each person their open tickets, soonest due first, what’s overdue and due this week across the company, and how each project, store, or product is doing. It’s on for every company and first in the sidebar, so it’s where Worklynq opens. See Home.

  • Open, overdue, and due-this-week counts
  • Needs you: my open tickets by due date
  • The busiest projects, stores, or products
  • Ask Worklynq, when AI Search is on

Tickets

Every piece of work, tracked from start to done.

Tickets hold everything your team works on: tasks, requests, issues, inspections, bugs. Each ticket type has its own workflow — a starting stage, up to five stages in the middle, and up to four end stages — and can run in a set order, move freely, or follow branches you design (see workflow types). Every end stage is marked as a success, a failure, or cancelled, so work can finish more than one way: a Change Order can end Approved, Rejected, or Withdrawn. The workflow editor draws several end stages as branches. Types can sit under one another, like a Story under an Epic or a Punch Item under a Milestone.

  • Ticket types with their own stages and colors
  • Several endings per type — success, failure, or cancelled
  • Parent and child tickets (Epic → Story, Milestone → Punch Item)
  • Sections you turn on per type: time logging, file uploads, parent / children, and GitHub, Claude, or Cursor
  • Assignees, priorities, due dates, comments, and attachments, plus fields suited to each kind of ticket — Occurred on and Corrective action on a food safety issue, Steps to reproduce on a bug
  • Find anything with Ask AI and Filters, and share saved filters with your team
  • Automations on each stage: create follow-up tickets, update fields, hand the ticket to Claude or Cursor, or run an AI agent
  • A library of 35 ready-made ticket types by industry

Many modules build on Tickets: Operations, Projects, Team Board, Roadmaps, Product Releases, and AI Agents.

Operations

Run every store, every day.

For work that never “finishes” — a café, a shop, a hotel, a kitchen, a site. Each store or site is a location with its own tickets, numbered with its own tag — DC-1, DC-2 at the Downtown café, RC-1 at Riverside. Your System Admin adds, renames, and archives locations in Admin console → Work Configuration → Locations. Operations has three tabs, each with its own section below: Calendar, Shifts and Daily brief. It’s also where you put a store on a TV with TV screens, and it’s what Inventory and Food safety build on.

  • One operation per store, site, or kitchen
  • Ticket numbers tagged by location, like DC-12
  • Only the ticket types you map to operations
  • On duty now: who’s working at each store, with Start my shift
  • A dashboard for each store
  • Recurring checklists that create themselves Coming in the beta

Managing locations. On the Locations page, click Add location and give it a name, a ticket tag (2–5 letters — Worklynq suggests one from the name, and you can change it), and an optional address or note. Worklynq then asks which ticket types the location uses, since its tickets can only use the types mapped to it. You can rename a location or change its tag later — tickets already made keep their numbers — and archive or restore it. Approvals keep their shared APR-1, APR-2 numbering, and tickets numbered OPS-… before tags arrived keep their numbers so links still work.

Needs: Tickets

Calendar

Everything happening today, hour by hour.

The first tab of Operations. It opens on the week, with each person’s photo on their tasks, and lays out each day’s work by time. Choose View by Location or Team member to see a column per store or per person, and pick one store or person to focus on; switch between day, week and month. Click an empty slot, or New task (which only offers your locations), to add a task, or click a task to mark it done, reopen it, or move it. Overdue tasks are flagged, done tasks get a tick, and each ticket type keeps its own color. A ticket with no time set shows on its due date.

  • Day, week, and month views
  • A column per store, or per person
  • Add a task in an empty slot, or schedule a ticket you already have
  • Done, overdue, and each ticket type in its own color
  • Every scheduled ticket shows its slot in the ticket itself

Part of Operations — it comes on with it

Shifts

Who’s on, who’s next, and how the week went.

The Shifts tab of Operations. Everyone gets My shifts: whether they’re on duty, their shifts for the next 30 days, and what they worked. Managers plan the team schedule week by week — add a shift, or Copy last week — and see each shift colored by how it went: on duty, worked, late, not in yet, or missed, with planned against worked hours. People start, pause and end their own shifts from the On duty now strip, or by telling Assist “start my shift”. If you use Square, starting and ending a shift also clocks the person in and out there.

  • My shifts, for everyone
  • Team schedule, upcoming, and completed shifts with CSV export
  • Start my shift, breaks, and End my shift — by tap or by voice
  • Late and missed shifts flagged
  • Clock-in and out on Square
  • Certifications checked when shifts are planned, with Training on
  • How Shifts works

Part of Operations — it comes on with it

Daily brief

Yesterday, every location, one screen.

The Daily brief tab of Operations shows yesterday — or any day you pick — across every location: sales against the same day last week, labour hours and sales per labour hour, food safety checks done, stock and tickets. At the top, up to six things that need you, most urgent first, each linking to the screen that fixes it; below, one card per location. Turn on Every morning to get it by email or as a phone notification at the time you choose. Each person only sees what they’re allowed to — sales stay with System Admins unless they’re opened to everyone.

  • Totals across all your locations
  • What needs you: up to six headlines, with links
  • A card per location, with who’s on today
  • Every morning by email or phone notification
  • Ask Assist “how did we do yesterday?”
  • How the Daily brief works

TV screens

The store’s live board, on any TV.

Put a store’s live orders, today’s tasks, and who’s on duty on a TV above the counter, in the kitchen, or in the back office. Click Show on TV in Operations or Order Tracking, pick what the TV shows, and type the code the TV displays at your workspace address followed by /tv. With more than one view, the TV switches between them, and a new order always comes to the front. A screen is read-only, needs no sign-in, shows one store only, and you can turn it off from Worklynq at any time. Managers see every screen and player in Operations → Screens & devices.

  • Order tracking: New, In progress, Ready for pickup — late orders in red
  • Operations today: each person’s tasks and checklists
  • Who’s on duty at the store
  • Connect with a 6-character code, or a link for signage apps
  • Worklynq Player: a box behind the TV, run from Worklynq Coming in the beta

Inventory

Know what’s on the shelves, and what to order.

Stock at each location, kept current with one tap — Count, + Received, Waste — or by telling Assist “we got 4 cases of tomatoes”. Worklynq reads what sold in Square and Order Tracking, works out daily usage through your menu links, and suggests what to order from each supplier and where to set your pars. On to start for Operations companies.

  • Stock by location, low and out first
  • Count mode for walking the walk-in
  • Order list by supplier, and par suggestions
  • Menu links: what one sale uses
  • How Inventory works

Needs: Operations

Food safety

Every temperature, logged and ready for the inspector.

Temperature and hygiene checks at each location on a schedule — coolers, freezer, hot holding, sanitizer, dish machine, opening checks — logged from a phone or by telling Assist “walk-in is 38”. A reading out of range asks what was done about it and opens a Food Safety Issue ticket for the manager. The report is the week-by-week log an inspector asks for, ready to print. On to start for Operations companies.

  • Standard checks with US Food Code limits, in °F or °C
  • Due, late and missed at a glance
  • Corrective actions that open a ticket
  • A printable inspector’s report
  • How Food safety works

Needs: Operations

Training

New hires trained, and every card current.

Courses for new hires — lessons, quizzes and sign-offs by a manager who watched — with a due date and progress for each person. Everyone’s certifications, like food handler cards, are tracked with warnings before they expire, and a manager can’t schedule someone whose required card will have expired.

  • A ready-made new hire course to start from
  • Lessons, quizzes and manager sign-offs
  • Certifications with expiry warnings, verified by a manager
  • Checked when shifts are planned, and in the Daily brief
  • How Training works

Projects

Every job, from kickoff to handover.

For work with a start, an end, and often a client — a job site, a renovation, a client engagement, a website. Anyone in the company can start a project, set its goals, milestones and deliverables, and follow it on a dashboard made for it. Projects is on for every kind of business, so a café can run its new-store opening the same way an agency runs a client job.

  • Goals, milestones with due dates, and deliverables — drafted by AI if you like
  • Public, or private to the project’s owners
  • A dashboard per project, which AI can change for you
  • Their own tickets and ticket tag
  • How Projects works

Needs: Tickets

Approvals

Get a yes or no, on the record.

For requests that need sign-off before they go ahead — documents, purchases, access, time off. Each type of request has its own stages, and every request ends approved, rejected, or withdrawn. Approved document approvals mark Knowledge Center pages as Verified. Optional: it’s off until your System Admin turns it on, whatever your business type, and it doesn’t need any other module.

  • Document, access, purchase, and time-off types ready to use
  • Add your own types, each with its own stages
  • Approved, rejected, and withdrawn endings
  • How Approvals works

Chat

Talk it through, then make it a ticket.

Company channels and private direct messages, with emoji reactions and profile photos. Select a few messages and click Make ticket or Make doc: the conversation goes with it, and the messages link to what they became. Optional: it’s off until your System Admin turns it on.

  • Channels for every store, crew, or project
  • Private direct messages
  • Messages become tickets or docs
  • Ask Assist to summarize a channel
  • How Chat works

Team Board

See the work. Keep everyone moving.

A shared board for planning and running work: keep a backlog, move tickets across the board as they progress, and plan in sprints if your team works that way.

  • Backlog planning with drag and drop
  • Board view by stage
  • Sprints
  • Sprint metrics: burndown, velocity and scope change
  • Filter by person, type, or priority

Sprint metrics, the third tab after Backlog and Kanban Board, shows how a team’s sprint is going. Pick the team and a sprint — the active one first, then completed ones — and count in tickets or story points:

  • Completed against the sprint’s current scope, what was committed at the start, the scope change (added and removed), and what’s remaining next to the team’s average per sprint.
  • Burndown: what’s left each day against an even burn of the commitment, with the scope line.
  • Velocity: committed against completed for the last six sprints and the active one, with the average of the last three.
  • By person (done and left for each assignee, and Unassigned), and the tickets added, removed or still open.

Past sprints are worked out from ticket history, so they show what really happened. Deleting a sprint moves its unfinished tickets back to the backlog; finished ones keep their sprint.

Needs: Tickets

Roadmaps

Turn plans into a timeline.

Lay out what's coming over the next weeks and months on one timeline — a product roadmap, a construction schedule, or next season's store openings. Each lane holds bars for the work, from start to end with how far along it is, and markers flag the dates that matter. Drag a bar to move it or change its dates; roadmaps can be public or private. Pinch with two fingers on a phone or tablet — or Ctrl/⌘ + scroll on a computer — to zoom the timeline in or out; phones open it at three months.

  • Lanes and bars on a timeline
  • Markers for key dates
  • Progress on every bar
  • Public or private, draft or published

Needs: Tickets

Product Releases

Ship with confidence.

Every product’s versions, soonest first: what’s in testing, what’s late, what shipped. Open a version for its own dashboard — progress, open and overdue work, who’s on what — and change it yourself or with AI. Tickets join a version through their Target release. System Admins add products in the Admin console; anyone can add versions. Mostly used by software teams.

  • Versions by product, with status and due dates
  • A dashboard per version
  • Release notes
  • How Product Releases works

Needs: Tickets

Dashboards

Your business at a glance.

Build dashboards from widgets — counts and KPIs, tickets by any field, today’s schedule, overdue work, completion, and trends — plus data from your connected tools, like Square sales, QuickBooks profit and loss, and your own software through custom apps. Keep them private, share them with a team, or with everyone, and point one at a single store, project, or product. Describe the dashboard you want and AI builds it, with a live preview before anything is saved; click Explain and AI tells you what the numbers say — using Worklynq’s own AI, or a Claude or OpenAI model your company has set up.

  • Dashboards built from widgets
  • Data from Square, QuickBooks, your own apps, and more
  • Shared with only you, a team, or everyone
  • One per store, project, or product — and every project and product version gets its own
  • Each widget in its own color, so they’re easy to tell apart
  • AI builds and explains them — or ask your own AI app
  • How Dashboards works

Needs nothing else

Reports

The numbers, in the inbox, before anyone asks.

Reports are sets of widgets made to be read and sent, with their own place in the sidebar. Open one any time, or email it every day, on weekdays, every week, or every month, at the time you choose. Each person gets their own copy, worked out as them, so they only see numbers they’re allowed to see — and every email starts with a 2–5 point AI summary of what changed, with the numbers underneath. Anyone who can see a report can also click Email me a copy to get it straight away.

  • Daily, weekdays, weekly, or monthly, in your time zone
  • The owner plus up to 25 people
  • An AI summary at the top of every email
  • Each copy shows only what that person may see
  • Last send: who got it, who was skipped, and why
  • How Reports work

Needs nothing else. Companies that had Dashboards get Reports too; with Reports off, scheduled emails pause and pick up again when it’s back on

Service

Knowledge Center

Your business's know-how, in one trusted place.

Knowledge Center keeps the things your team needs to know — how you open the store, how you run a job, how the product works — written down, approved, and easy to find. It's also what Worklynq's AI reads, so its answers are based on how your business actually works.

Spaces

Write it down once. Share it with the right people.

Your own My Space plus shared spaces for the company, all in one module — pick a space at the top of the page tree, with My Space first. Write documents, notes, and procedures in a rich editor, start from templates, send important documents for approval (with the Approvals module on), and archive what's out of date without losing it. Archive and Templates sit under Spaces in the sidebar while it’s open, and each has its own section below.

  • My Space and shared spaces, with one space picker
  • Rich editor, and templates to start from
  • Verified pages, with Approvals
  • Version history
  • Archive that stays searchable, and Docs trash for anything deleted
  • Pages shared with you stay private to the people they’re shared with
  • Ask Assist to summarize the page you’re reading

Page covers: give a page a cover photo or gradient from 107 photos and 36 gradients, grouped into rows — Landscapes, Water, Sky & stars, Cities & places, Work & building, Food, shops & stays, Art & texture, then gradients from light to dark — or upload your own. Hover a cover to see its name. The same picker sets a page’s footer.

Coming back to Spaces from another module reopens the last page you had open; the Knowledge Library does the same. On a phone, once a page is open the page tree folds behind a Pages button, so the page gets the whole screen.

Templates

Never start from a blank page.

Templates, under Spaces in the sidebar, has 38 templates written for small businesses in any industry, in nine categories: Operations, Team & HR, Customers & Sales, Marketing, Finance & Admin, Safety & Compliance, Meetings & Planning, Product, and IT & Tech. They include an opening & closing checklist, a shift handover, a stock count, a risk assessment, a quote & proposal and a social media calendar. Each has its own picture, and its sections come out as numbered, colored cards — in the catalog, in pages made from it, and in the editor. Pick one and click Use Template, or + New to save your own. Save to lists My Space, every shared space and the folders inside them, so it saves where you want it.

Starting from a new, blank page? Browse all templates opens Templates for that page: pick one and click Use on my page, and it fills in the page you started — in the folder you made it in — then takes you back to it. ← Back to the page and ← Templates are always there, and Worklynq asks before dropping anything you’ve filled in.

  • 38 ready-made templates in nine categories
  • Numbered, colored sections
  • Save your own company templates

Part of Spaces — it comes on with it

Archive & Docs trash

Out of the way, never lost.

Archive, under Spaces in the sidebar, holds pages that are out of date but worth keeping — last year’s menu, an old procedure. Archived pages leave the page tree but stay searchable and readable; click Restore to edit one again.

Deleted something by mistake? Deleting a page, a folder, or a whole space moves it to trash rather than erasing it. Your System Admin opens Admin console → Work Configuration → Docs trash and clicks Recover to put it back where it was, or Delete to remove it for good.

  • Archive pages without losing them
  • Archived pages stay searchable
  • Deleted pages, folders and spaces wait in Docs trash
  • Recover puts them back where they were

Part of Spaces — it comes on with it

Knowledge Library

The company handbook that's always current.

Curated collections of your most important documents: policies, procedures, guides, playbooks, and standards. People find answers without asking around, and AI uses the Library as trusted context.

  • Collections chosen for your kind of business
  • Policies and procedures (SOPs)
  • Guides, playbooks, and standards
  • Searchable by everyone
  • Trusted context for AI

Set it up for your business. The first time someone opens the Knowledge Library, it asks two things:

  1. What kind of business are you? Pick one or more of 19 — from restaurants, shops and hotels to clinics, trades, agencies, software and nonprofits. Your company’s industry is already picked.
  2. Choose your collections. 105 collections across Business Areas, Company Wide Docs, Playbooks, Policies and SOPs, each with a picture. The ones that fit your business are already ticked. Filter by Suggested, All or Selected, search, add your own to any section, then click Create my library.

Change your mind any time with Choose collections, in the library’s header and its sidebar: add suggestions, or remove collections that are still empty. A collection with documents in it is never removed. Libraries set up before this keep their collections and skip the setup.

Pages open like Spaces. Click a page in a collection and it opens in view mode, with the sidebar still there and a breadcrumb at the top (Knowledge Library › section › collection › page). Edit Page edits it in place; Done Editing saves it, Cancel drops the changes. The ⋯ menu exports it as PDF or Word, moves it to a space, or moves it to trash.

Needs: Spaces

Service

AI Hub

AI that works alongside your team.

AI Hub brings AI into the work instead of bolting a chatbot onto it. Ask questions across your business and put AI agents to work — while people stay in charge of the decisions. AI also lives where the data is: it builds and explains dashboards and reports, and your team can use Worklynq from the AI apps they already have.

AI Search

Ask a question. Get an answer, with sources.

Ask in plain language — “What's still open at the downtown store?” or “What did we decide about the supplier change?” — and get an answer drawn from your tickets, documents, and connected tools, with links back to where it came from. You only see answers from what you're allowed to see: AI Search checks your roles before it searches each source, never shows other people’s personal pages, and only searches your own Gmail if you connected it.

Web Search next to the question box searches the web as well, for questions about the outside world — a supplier, a regulation, a competitor. Paste a link (or just a domain, like example.com) and AI Search reads the page and answers from it together with your workspace; Attach Files does the same with a document. The sources come as a numbered list under the answer.

  • Plain-language questions
  • Answers with links to the source
  • Web Search, pasted links and attached files
  • Respects everyone's permissions
  • Across tickets, documents, and connected tools
  • Assist: the same AI in a panel on every page

Needs nothing else. Assist comes with it

Assist

Ask, open, or get it done — from any page.

A chat panel on the side of every page, Spaces and the Knowledge Library included. Ask about your work (“Which tickets are overdue?”), tell it what to open (“Show me DC-42”), or have it do things: create and update tickets, write pages, build dashboards, log a fridge temperature, count stock, or start your shift. It knows what’s on your screen, so “summarize this page” or “what’s this ticket waiting on?” just work. Type or talk. Assist works as you, with your permissions, and asks Go ahead before it changes anything — except starting, pausing or ending your own shift.

  • On every page, beside the work instead of over it
  • Answers about the page, ticket, dashboard or channel you’re looking at
  • Does things across modules — tickets, docs, dashboards, orders, shifts, stock, food safety, training
  • Asks before it changes anything, then links every ticket it changed
  • Drop a file on it — Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF or CSV — to make pages, tickets or a summary
  • Voice, and a searchable History
  • How Assist works

Part of AI Search — anyone whose role can use AI Search can use Assist

AI Agents

Every AI agent working in Worklynq, in one place.

Set up agents that look things up and, when you allow it, create and update tickets and docs — as the person who runs them, never with more access. Run one yourself, on a schedule, or when a ticket reaches a stage, and choose whether it asks before making changes.
