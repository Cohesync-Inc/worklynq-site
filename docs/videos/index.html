Video tutorials

Short narrated videos that show you around Worklynq, plus an Admin console series for System Admins. Each one is filmed in the app with a demo café group, Harbor & Pine, so you see exactly what you’ll click. Captions are available with the CC button. We add new videos as we go.

Your first look at Worklynq

Two minutes on where things are: Home and what needs you, the sidebar and its modules, opening a ticket, and asking Assist a question, then having it log a ticket for you. Read more in Home, Tickets and Assist.

Transcript

Welcome to Worklynq. In the next two minutes, you’ll get your bearings: your Home page, the sidebar, your tickets, and Assist, your built-in AI helper.

When you sign in, you land on Home. It greets you with today’s date and your company, so you always know where you are.

Across the top are four numbers: open tickets across the company, anything overdue, what’s due this week, and what’s assigned to you.

Below them, Needs you lists the work waiting on you, with its status and when it’s due. Anything late is flagged in red.

Further down, each of your locations shows how many tickets are open, so the busy ones stand out. Depending on how your workspace is set up, you’ll see your projects, products and approvals here too.

On the left is the sidebar. Each icon opens a module, like Operations, Inventory, Tickets and Projects. Expand it any time to see their names.

Your sidebar only shows the modules your company has turned on, so yours may look a little different from this one.

Most of the work in Worklynq is tracked as tickets. Each ticket type has its own fields and stages, set up for the way your business works. Click one to open it.

You’ll see what kind of ticket it is, who reported it, where it is, and its status. Below that, who it’s assigned to, its priority, and when it’s due.

When you’re done, close it, and you’re back in your ticket list.

Now for Assist. It’s an AI helper that’s there on every screen. Open it with the Assist button.

Ask a question in plain English, like: what’s still waiting at Downtown?

Assist looks across your tickets, pages and info from your connected tools, and answers in seconds. It even tells you which one to chase first.

Assist can do things for you, too. Ask it to log a ticket, and it fills in the details for you.

Before it changes anything, Assist shows you exactly what it’s about to do, and waits for your go-ahead.

Click Go ahead, and the ticket is created, with a link straight to it.

That’s the tour. Next up: working with tickets. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Working with tickets

Two minutes on everyday ticket work: the Tickets list, creating a ticket in the right work area with its type’s own fields, moving it through its stages, finding tickets with Ask AI, and building, saving and sharing filters. Read more in Tickets.

Transcript

In this video, you’ll create a ticket, move it along as the work gets done, and find the tickets you need with filters and Ask AI.

Open Tickets from the sidebar. You’ll see every ticket you have access to, across your whole workspace.

Each row shows the ticket’s number and type, its title, when it’s due, its status, who it’s assigned to, and its work area.

To log something new, click the plus button at the top of the sidebar. It’s there on every screen.

First, pick the work area, like a location, project or product. Then choose the ticket type. Each work area offers its own types.

Give it a title, then fill in the fields that come with its type. A maintenance request asks who’s on it, its priority, when it’s due, and the vendor.

Click Create Ticket, and it’s saved with its own number, ready for the assignee.

To update a ticket, open it. Here’s the leaking espresso machine from this morning.

Change its status as the work moves along. Each ticket type has its own stages. For a repair, that’s Reported, Scheduled, In Progress, and Fixed.

You can also reassign it, change its priority or due date, and add a comment for your team. Every change is saved as you go.

Looking for something? Describe it in plain English in the Ask AI box, like: open high priority tickets at both cafés.

Worklynq turns it into a filter, and shows each part as a chip, so you can see exactly what it searched for.

Or open Filters to build your own. Start from a quick filter, like Overdue or Urgent open, or add conditions one at a time.

Save the filters you use often, and share them with your team if they need them too. Your saved filters are always one click away.

That’s tickets. Next up: getting more out of Assist. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Getting more out of Assist

What you can ask Assist, how it gets things done once you say Go ahead, and asking about whatever’s on your screen. Read more in Assist.

Transcript

Assist is the AI helper on every page of Worklynq. In this video, you’ll see what you can ask, how it gets things done for you, and how it answers questions about whatever’s on your screen.

Open Assist with the button at the bottom right. It sits beside the page, and stays open as you move around.

Ask about your work in plain English. Assist answers from everything you’re allowed to see, and it only tells you what it actually found. It never makes up tickets, names or numbers.

Assist also knows what’s on your screen. Here’s our opening and closing procedures page in Spaces. Just ask Assist to summarize this page.

It reads the page you have open, and answers right beside it. The same works for a ticket, a dashboard, a chat channel, or a list of tickets.

You can also ask Assist to get things done, like creating or updating tickets, writing pages, or building dashboards.

Looking things up happens straight away. But before Assist changes anything, it shows you exactly what it’s about to do, and waits. Click Go ahead, or Don’t.

Once it’s done, you get a card for every ticket it changed, with a link straight to it.

Every chat is saved. Open History to find past chats. They’re grouped by day, and you can search them or filter by module.

And when your hands are busy, tap the microphone and just say it, like start my shift, or walk-in is 38 degrees.

That’s Assist. Next up: signing in, your profile, and notifications. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Signing in, your profile and notifications

Signing in and resetting a password, setting up your profile, your notifications, and the email digest. Read more in Signing in.

Transcript

In this video: how to sign in, what to do if you forget your password, how to set up your profile, and how Worklynq keeps you up to date.

Everyone signs in at one address: login.worklynq.net. Enter your email and password, and Worklynq takes you straight to your company’s workspace.

Forgot your password? Click Forgot password, and enter your email. We’ll send you a link to choose a new one. It works once, for 30 minutes.

Once you’re in, set up your profile. Click your initials at the bottom of the sidebar, then Profile.

Add a photo, so your team can see who’s who, and check your name and location. Your email is the one you sign in with, so it can’t be changed here.

Notifications tell you when a ticket is assigned to you, or when someone mentions you in a comment. Open them from the same menu.

Click one to jump straight to the ticket. And when you’re caught up, Mark all read.

Prefer a summary? Your profile also has a Digest: a short brief of what needs your decision, what’s blocked, and what changed.

Choose daily or weekly, and the hour it arrives. It comes to you by email.

When you’re done, click your initials and Log out. You’re signed out everywhere, so on a shared computer, the next person can’t walk back in.

That’s signing in, your profile and notifications. Next up: finding anything with AI Search. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Finding anything with AI Search

Ask AI Search a question in plain English and get a short answer with its sources, then find past searches in the sidebar.

Transcript

AI Search answers questions from everything in your workspace: tickets, orders, pages, and the tools you’ve connected. Here’s how to use it.

Open AI Search from the sidebar, and ask a question in plain English. For example: which food safety issues did we have this month, and are any still open?

AI Search looks through your tickets, docs and connected tools, and only uses what you’re allowed to see.

You get a short answer, with the sources it used underneath. Here, three food safety tickets, and the opening and closing procedures page. Click a source to open it.

Every search is saved. Open the sidebar to find past chats, search them, or keep them in folders. Your Assist chats are saved here too, so you can pick up where you left off.

You can also attach a file or a link and ask about it, or turn on Web Search to look beyond your workspace.

That’s it for Getting started. You’ll find more videos, and every guide, at worklynq.com/docs.

Signing up and first-time setup

From sign-up to a workspace your team can use: your email and address, a plan, then the six setup steps. Read more in Getting started.

Transcript

This video is for the person setting up Worklynq for their company. You’ll sign up, choose a plan, and then fit your workspace to your business in six short steps.

Start by creating your workspace. Enter your work email, and we’ll send you a six-digit code.

Type the code from the email to confirm it’s you.

Next, choose your workspace address. It’s checked as you type, and it can’t be changed later, so pick one your team will recognize.

Then tell us about your company and you: what kind of company it is, your industry, your team size, and a password.

Last, choose your plan: Basic, Advance, or Premium, monthly or yearly, and how many people. You pay on Stripe’s secure checkout page.

Once your payment goes through, Worklynq builds your own private workspace. It usually takes a few minutes. You can close the page, and we’ll email you when it’s ready.

The first time you sign in, setup opens. Only you see it, and you can finish later at any time. First, confirm the kind of company and your industry. That decides which modules we turn on, and which ticket types we suggest.

Next, pick the modules you’ll use. We’ve switched on what companies like yours use most. Untick anything you don’t need, so your team sees a simpler menu.

Then add where your work happens. For a restaurant, that’s your locations. Add one now, and the rest any time.

Choose your ticket types. The ones made for your industry are ticked, each with its stages from start to finish. Untick what you won’t use.

Connect the tools you already use, like Square or QuickBooks. Each one asks you to sign in to that tool, and nothing is shared until you approve it.

Finally, invite your team. Choose Admin for people who manage settings, or Member for everyone else. They’ll get an email with a link to join.

Click Send invites and finish, and you’re in. Everything you chose can be changed later in the Admin console.

That’s sign-up and setup. Next in the Admin console series: a tour of the Admin console. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

A tour of the Admin console

Where to find people, billing, how work is set up, connected apps and AI usage.

Transcript

The Admin console is where System Admins run the workspace: people, billing, how work is set up, connected apps, and AI usage. Here’s a quick tour.

Open it from your account menu, by choosing System Admin. Only System Admins see this option.

The sidebar groups everything into five areas. User Management is where you add people, organize them into teams, and set what each role can do.

Billing has your subscription, your invoices, and extra AI credit packs.

Work Configuration is how work is set up: the modules you use, your products, projects and locations, fields and ticket types, Spaces, approval types, and trash for anything deleted.

Integrations is where you connect apps like Square, QuickBooks and GitHub.

And AI Monitoring shows how many AI credits your company has used this month, who’s using them, and on what.

That’s the Admin console. Next in the series: inviting people and managing users. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Inviting people and managing users

Invite people, follow up on invitations, and change or remove someone’s access.

Transcript

In this video: how to invite people to your workspace, follow up on invitations, and change or remove someone’s access.

In the Admin console, Users lists everyone in your workspace, with their role, their status, and when they were last active.

To invite someone, click Add User. Enter their name and work email, and pick a role.

System Admins can do everything, including billing. Admins manage people, teams and settings. Everyone else is a Tenant, with standard access to their work.

Click Invite User. They’ll get an email with a link to join, and until they accept, they show here as a pending invitation.

Invitations last 14 days. If someone hasn’t joined yet, open the menu on their row to resend the invitation, or cancel it.

To change someone’s details or role, open the menu on their row, and choose Edit user.

Here you can update their name, or change their role. If someone leaves, deactivate them. They can’t sign in any more, but everything they worked on stays.

That’s managing users. Next in the Admin console series: teams and roles. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Teams and roles

Group people who work together, and control what each role can do.

Transcript

In this video: grouping people into teams, and controlling what each role can do.

Teams group people who work together, like the crew at each of your locations. Each team shows its manager and its members.

Teams are used across Worklynq, for example to share a space in Spaces with the right people. Click a team to see who’s in it, add someone, or remove them.

To start a new team, click New Team, and give it a name, a short description, and a color.

Roles control what people can do. Worklynq comes with three: System Admin, Admin and Tenant. To give some people just the access they need, create your own role with New role, like this one for shift leads.

Click Edit to see a role’s permissions. The ones on the right are on the role, and the ones on the left aren’t. Move them across to give or take away access, then save.

Billing is the one exception. Only the System Admin sees it, or a role of your own that you give billing access to.

That’s teams and roles. Next in the Admin console series: turning modules on and off. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Modules: turning features on and off

Choose the parts of Worklynq your company uses, and their order in the sidebar. Read more in Modules.

Transcript

Modules are the parts of Worklynq your company uses, like Operations, Inventory, Tickets or Projects. Here’s how to choose them.

In the Admin console, open Work Configuration, then Modules. Each module has a switch, and a short line on what it does.

Turn a module off, and once you save, it’s hidden from everyone’s sidebar. Its data is kept, so you can turn it back on at any time.

On the right, you can see exactly what your team will see. Some modules need another one, like Projects needs Tickets, and each row tells you when that’s the case.

Drag a row, or use the arrows, to change the order modules appear in the sidebar. Then click Save changes.

And if you want to start over, Reset to recommended puts back the modules made for your kind of company.

That’s modules. Next in the Admin console series: setting up your locations, projects and products. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Locations, projects and products

Set up where your work happens, with a ticket tag and ticket types for each location.

Transcript

Work in Worklynq always happens somewhere: at a location, in a project, or for a product. In this video, you’ll set those up in the Admin console.

Under Work Configuration, Locations lists the places your team works. Every operations ticket belongs to a location, and its number starts with the location’s ticket tag, like DC for Downtown café.

To add one, click Add location, and give it a name. Worklynq suggests a short ticket tag from the name, and you can add an address or a note for your team.

Next, choose which ticket types people can create there. You can change this any time, in Ticket Type Mapping.

Each location shows how many ticket types it uses. And when a location closes, archive it. Its tickets and history are kept.

Projects are work with a start and an end, like opening a new café, or launching a winter menu. Each one shows its objective, its progress, and its timeline.

You can also start projects from the Projects module, where Assist can draft the plan for you. And if your company builds software, your products are set up here too, once Product Releases is on.

That’s where your work happens. Next in the Admin console series: ticket types and custom workflows. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Ticket types and custom workflows

Stages, workflow types, end-stage outcomes, and branching workflows. Read more in How tickets move.

Transcript

This video is for System Admins. You’ll see how ticket types work, how to set up a type’s stages, and how to build a custom workflow that can branch and loop back.

Open the Admin console from your account menu, by choosing System Admin.

Ticket types live under Work Configuration. Click Ticket Types.

Each ticket type is a kind of work your team tracks, like a maintenance request, a checklist or a menu change. The list shows how its tickets move, its stages, and the sections it uses.

Click a type to edit it. Let’s open Menu Change. You can set its name, icon and description, and whether it’s a top-level type, or a child that sits under a parent.

Next, choose how its tickets move. Linear goes one stage at a time. Free flow can jump from any stage to any other. And Custom lets you decide where each stage can go next.

Then set up the stages: a start, up to five in the middle, and up to four end stages, each with its own name and color. A stage can also run automations when a ticket enters or leaves it.

Every end stage has an outcome: success, failure, or cancelled. So a menu change can finish Approved or Rejected, and Home and your reports know which way it went.

With a custom workflow, Branches lists each stage, with the stages it can move to next. Here, Tasting can go on to Costed, back for changes, or straight to Rejected.

The diagram draws your workflow as you go: moves forward as solid lines, and moves back as dashed ones.

Click Save. Worklynq checks that every stage can be reached and has somewhere to go next, so tickets never get stuck.

On a ticket, the status menu follows your workflow. This menu change has been costed, so it can only move on to Approved or Rejected. The other stages are greyed out.

That’s ticket types and workflows. Next in the Admin console series: the fields and sections on each ticket type. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.

Fields and ticket type screens

Add your own fields, and choose which ones each ticket type shows. Read more in Fields for each type of ticket.

Transcript

Every ticket type asks for the details that fit its kind of work. In this video, you’ll add your own fields, and choose which ones each ticket type shows.

Fields are the details a ticket asks for, like its assignee, its due date, or the vendor for a repair. You’ll find them in Work Configuration, under Fields.

System fields come built in. Custom Fields are your own. Here, the café tracks the vendor and cost of a repair, and the temperature reading on a food safety issue.

To add one, click New Custom Field, give it a name, and choose its type, like text, a number, a date, or a dropdown.

Then choose where each field shows. In Ticket Type Screens, pick a ticket type, like Food Safety Issue, and a screen: Create, or Edit and View.

Each type starts from a recommended layout for its kind of work. Switch a field on to show it, and mark it required if it must always be filled in. Then save your changes.

That’s fields and screens. Next in the Admin console series: sections, like time logging and file uploads. You’ll find every guide at worklynq.com/docs.
