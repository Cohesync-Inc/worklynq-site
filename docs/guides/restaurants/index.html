Industry guide

Restaurants & cafés

From opening checklists to supplier orders and guest feedback, here’s how a café or restaurant runs its week in Worklynq. The example is a two-location café — Downtown and Riverside — with an owner, two shift leads, and a small crew.

Prefer to watch? Follow Harbor & Pine through one day, from the morning brief to closing. More are on Video tutorials.

Built around Operations. The Operations module gives each store its own tickets and a day, week, and month calendar, and each store can have its own dashboard. Shifts, the Daily brief and TV screens are part of it. Checklists that repeat on their own Coming in the beta; until then, add each day’s opening and closing checklist to the calendar as a ticket.

Recommended setup

A week in Worklynq

  1. Mon

    Opening up

    The shift lead walks in and tells Assist “start my shift” — they’re on duty in Worklynq and clocked in on Square. Today’s Opening checklist is on the Operations calendar for each store, assigned to the shift lead, in its own color so it never gets mixed up with one-off requests. Each item — set up the espresso machine, count the till — is ticked off inside the ticket. At 6 AM the walk-in reading goes into Food safety — “walk-in is 38”, to Assist — and the inspector’s log fills itself. Watch: Shifts · Watch: Food safety

  2. Tue

    The ice machine breaks

    A barista opens a Maintenance Request from their phone at Riverside, adds a photo, and the owner sees it on the store’s board. When the technician’s visit is booked, the date goes on the ticket so everyone knows. Watch: Working with tickets

  3. Wed

    Supplier orders

    The shift lead opens Inventory: the order list says what to order from each supplier, worked out from what sold in Square this week. They create the weekly Supplier Order for milk and pastries, and when it arrives, one tap on Received books it into stock. Watch: Inventory

  4. Thu

    A guest complaint

    A guest emails about a cold sandwich. It becomes Guest Feedback with the details, the owner replies, and the follow-up is tracked to done — nothing slips.

  5. Fri

    Covering the weekend

    Someone calls in sick for Saturday. A Staffing Request goes out, a teammate picks it up, and the manager swaps them into the team schedule. Everyone sees their next shift in My shifts. Watch: Shifts

  6. Sat

    A catering order

    A regular orders coffee and pastries for 30 for Monday morning. The barista adds it in Order Tracking — items, pickup, ready by 8:00 — and it lands in the New column. The kitchen moves it to In progress on Monday, then Ready, and the kitchen TV shows it moving from New to Ready for pickup, next to the café’s Square orders. Watch: Order Tracking · Watch: TV screens

  7. Sun

    The owner’s view

    The Daily brief lands at 6:30 every morning: yesterday’s sales against last week, labour hours, food safety, what to order, and the few things to act on first. On Sunday the owner also opens Inventory → Profit and raises the price on the one dish that costs too much to make. Watch: Daily brief

Your first week

A System Admin can set a café up in an afternoon. In this order:

  1. Sign up and add your locations, one per store. Watch
  2. Invite the team and give shift leads a role that can plan shifts. Watch
  3. Connect Square and match each Square location to its store, so sales reach the brief, inventory and dashboards. Add QuickBooks if you use it. Watch
  4. Add the standard food safety checks for each store, in °F or °C. Watch
  5. Add your stock items and pars, then link your best-selling menu items to what they use. Watch
  6. Add the new hire course and the food handler card, and assign the course to anyone new. Watch
  7. Plan next week’s shifts; after that, Copy last week does most of it. Watch
  8. Put a TV above the counter with Order tracking and Who’s on duty. Watch
  9. Turn on the Daily brief for yourself and your managers. Watch

Tips

  • Write your opening and closing procedures in Knowledge Center once — new staff can read them, and AI Search can answer questions from them.
  • Use a Food Safety Issue for anything health-related, so it has its own stages and nothing gets lost in general tasks.
  • Keep checklist items short and specific: “Fridge 1 below 41°F” beats “Check fridges.”
  • Put a TV above the pass showing Order tracking and Who’s on duty — see TV screens.
  • Use the Walk-in button for counter orders so nobody has to look up a customer.
  • Count the walk-in once a week in Inventory’s count mode — the more you count, the better the order list and par suggestions get.
  • Add the standard food safety checks on day one; print the report before an inspection.
  • Plan the week’s posts in Social — write one, and AI writes the Instagram and Google versions.
  • Turn on Chat with a channel per store. When a message is really a job, select it and click Make ticket.
