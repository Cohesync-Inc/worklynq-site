Industry guide

IT services & agencies

Here’s how an IT services company runs its client work in Worklynq — fixed-bid builds, dedicated teams and support retainers side by side, with the code in GitHub and every client’s status a click away. The example is Vector6 Tech, a 30-person company in Pune with five clients, four delivery pods, a support desk and 20 GitHub repositories.

Built around Projects. Each client engagement is a project with its own ticket prefix, milestones, sprints and dashboard. If you also build and look after an app for a client, add it as a product so each version gets its own release dashboard. Teams are your pods, and roles decide who sees client contracts and billing.

Ticket types for client work

Each type has its own stages, so a client’s request and a sprint story don’t share one generic workflow:

TypeUse it forStages
Service RequestA client asks for help or a change on a support retainerNew › Assigned › In Progress › Waiting on Client › Resolved
Change RequestA change to the agreed scope or a client’s systems, which needs the client’s OKRequested › Under Review › Approved › Scheduled › Implemented
IncidentSomething is down or broken for the client’s users right nowReported › Investigating › Mitigated › Resolved
MilestoneA phase or payment milestone in the contractPlanned › In Progress › At Risk › Complete
Epic, Story, Task, BugThe delivery work itself, planned in sprintsStory: To Do › In Progress › In Review › QA › Done

A week in Worklynq

  1. Mon

    Delivery review

    The Head of Delivery opens the Delivery Overview dashboard: what’s open and late on every client project, and where this week’s hours are going. Each pod lead has their own client dashboard too — Northwind’s shows the Black Friday code freeze and hypercare. Trackfold is flagged at risk, so the Trackfold pod lead plans the recovery in this sprint on the Team Board. Watch: Dashboards · Watch: Team Board

  2. Tue

    A P1 on a retainer

    Greenleaf’s grade export is timing out for every school. The support desk logs an Incident (GLL-31, critical) and moves it to Mitigated once exports run again; the engineers log their time on the ticket, so the retainer’s hours this month stay accurate on Greenleaf’s support dashboard. Everyday requests — “rename Learning Areas to Subjects” — are Service Requests that wait in Waiting on Client when the ball is in the client’s court. Watch: Working with tickets

  3. Wed

    A change request

    Northwind asks for Afterpay at checkout. Instead of slipping into the sprint, it’s a Change Request (NWO-63, “CR-02”) with the estimate and impact. The Client Partner moves it through Under Review to Approved once Northwind signs, and only then is it scheduled. The client’s record in Customers shows the account and its owner, and Engagement keeps every call and meeting about it. Watch: Customers

  4. Thu

    Code and tickets in one place

    An engineer on the Medora pod picks up the bug MDH-36, “Large text breaks appointment card layout”. With the GitHub section turned on for Bugs, the ticket shows the branch, the commits and the open pull request — reviewers don’t have to ask where the fix is. If you’ve connected Claude or Cursor, small, well-defined bugs can go to an agent instead, and come back as a pull request your team reviews. Read: GitHub

  5. Fri

    Client status, emailed

    The Weekly Client Status report — completed this week, hours logged by project, overdue work, milestones due in the next 7 days, and anything critical or at risk — lands in the leadership team’s inbox at a set time, with a short AI summary on top. Each person gets their own copy, worked out as them. Watch: Scheduled reports

  6. Anytime

    Ask about the account

    Every client has a space in Spaces: the SOW, runbooks, meeting notes, status reports, the RAID log and the change request log. Before a call, the Client Partner asks AI Search “What did we agree with Trackfold about the battery drain?” and gets the answer from last week’s meeting notes, with a link. Watch: AI Search · Watch: Spaces

Your first week

A System Admin can set up an IT services company in a day. In this order:

  1. Sign up and choose Projects as your business type and IT services as your industry. Watch
  2. Invite the team, add a team per pod, and give each person a role: engineers work tickets, while client partners and finance also see Customers and billing. Watch · Watch
  3. Add a project per client engagement, with its own ticket prefix (NWO, MDH…), owners and milestones. Plan with Assist drafts the milestones from the SOW. Watch
  4. Add the client-work ticket types — Service Request, Change Request, Incident and Milestone — and turn on time logging for the types you bill. Watch · Watch
  5. Connect GitHub, choose the repositories, and turn the GitHub section on for Story, Task, Bug and the other delivery types. Watch
  6. Add each client in Customers, with their contacts, subscriptions and the account owner, and log calls and meetings in Engagement. Watch
  7. Make a space per client for the SOW, runbooks and meeting notes. Watch
  8. Build a dashboard per client and a weekly status report, and schedule it for Friday. Watch

Tips

  • Keep one project per engagement, not per client: a client with a build and a separate support retainer gets two projects, so hours and dashboards don’t mix.
  • Use a Change Request for anything outside the agreed scope, so the client’s OK is recorded before the work starts — and the CR log writes itself.
  • Put retainer limits and SLAs in the project’s description and on its dashboard (for example “120 h/month, P1 response 1 h”), so the whole pod sees them.
  • Write meeting notes in the client’s space straight after each call. AI Search answers from them, and only people who can see that space get those answers.
  • Click Explain on a client dashboard before a status call — it tells you what changed in plain words.
  • Use Chat with a channel per client. When a message is really a job, select it and click Make ticket.
  • Building a product of your own rather than client work? See the software teams guide.
