Tickets

Every piece of work in Worklynq is a ticket — a checklist, a repair, a supplier order, an RFI, a bug. This page covers finding and filtering tickets, creating them, editing many at once, and setting up each type of ticket: the fields it asks for and how it moves from start to finish.

The tickets list

  • The list fills your screen. Columns are sized to be readable, and the Title takes the rest of the width.
  • Drag a column’s edge to resize it — including making the Title narrower, so long titles are cut short. Your widths are remembered. Double-click a column’s edge to put it back to automatic width.
  • On a smaller screen, or with Assist open beside the list, the Title keeps a readable width first, and the Created and Updated dates step aside until there’s room. Your own column settings don’t change.
  • Sort by any column, and click a ticket to open it.
  • Filter opens the Filters panel: describe what you want, pick a quick filter, build conditions, and manage your saved filters.

Search with Ask AI

Type what you’re looking for in plain words in the Ask AI box in the toolbar (on a narrow screen it gets its own row under Group, Filter, Columns, and Bulk edit) — for example “my open repairs due this week”, “all operations tickets”, or “overdue at Downtown” — and press Enter. Worklynq turns it into filter conditions and shows the matching tickets.

A bar under the toolbar shows exactly what was applied, as chips — ✦ AI applied: Unresolved · Downtown café · Priority: Critical, High. Then:

  • Click × on a chip to drop that condition.
  • Show query reveals the search as a Worklynq Query, with a Copy button.
  • Edit opens the conditions in the Filters panel to adjust them; Save keeps them as a saved filter; Clear goes back to the full list.
  • “Open” means not finished. Ask AI reads open and closed from each ticket type’s workflow: a ticket is resolved once it reaches one of its type’s end stages, whatever that stage is called.
  • It knows your work. It only uses your real statuses, ticket types, work areas, sprints, and releases, and it understands kinds of work — “operations tickets” means the tickets at all your locations.
  • If AI isn’t available, Worklynq searches by keyword instead and says so.

Filters

Click Filter in the toolbar. Everything is on one page:

  1. Describe it. Type what you want, like “urgent bugs nobody owns”, and click Fill in — Ask AI fills in the conditions below for you to check.
  2. Quick filters: My open, Unassigned, Overdue, Due this week, Urgent open, Created this week, and Reported by me.
  3. Conditions. Choose to show tickets matching all or any of your conditions. Each one reads like a sentence — a field, then is, is not, is empty, contains, in the last / next N days, before, after, between, and so on — and you pick values from your real statuses, people, work areas, teams, sprints, releases, and fields, including your own custom fields. + Add group nests a block with its own all/any, for example assigned to me AND (priority is High or Critical OR overdue).
  4. The footer shows how many tickets match as you go. View as query shows the Worklynq Query; Save as… names the filter and shares it with Only me or Everyone; Apply shows the tickets.

Saved filters sit at the bottom of the panel, under Mine and Shared with me, each with its conditions in plain words. Click one to apply it. Its menu lets you Edit it (then Update filter or Save as new), Rename, Share with everyone or Make private, Duplicate, Copy query, or Delete; renaming, sharing and deleting are for your own filters. Click the ★ to make a filter your default.

Know the query language? A saved filter or link with a hand-written Worklynq Query still works, even when it’s too complex to show as conditions — the panel shows it as a read-only query.

Create a ticket

Click + at the top of the sidebar, or New ticket on a page. Pick the Work area — the store, project, product, or approval space the work belongs to — then the Ticket type. Only the types mapped to that work area are offered. If you see No work area available, you haven’t been given access to any yet — ask your administrator.

The rest of the form asks for the fields that suit that type of ticket — see Fields for each type of ticket.

Your System Admin chooses which ticket types each work area can use in Admin console → Work Configuration → Ticket Type Mapping.

Edit many tickets at once

Super admins can change several tickets together — for example, move a week’s checklists to Done or reassign a batch of repairs.

  1. On the tickets list, turn on Bulk edit. Checkboxes appear.
  2. Tick the first ticket. Only similar tickets — the same work area and the same ticket type — stay selectable; the rest are dimmed.
  3. Tick the others, or select all similar tickets on the page.
  4. Choose what to change: status (from that type’s workflow), priority, assignee, due date, and, for products, sprint and release. Every field starts at Keep as is.
  5. Save. Worklynq updates every selected ticket.

Fields for each type of ticket

Every ticket type asks for the fields that fit its kind of work, and nothing else. A food safety issue asks when it happened and who reported it; a bug asks for the environment and steps to reproduce; an approval asks for the amount. Title and Status are always there, and * marks a required field.

Worklynq picks the kind from the ticket type’s name, its description, and the work areas it’s used in. A Task on a software product gets the engineering fields; a Task at a café gets the general ones.

Change the fields

Your System Admin can turn any field on or off, or make it required, in Admin console → Work Configuration → Ticket Type Screens: pick the ticket type, then the Create or Edit / View screen.

  • A type nobody has set up yet uses its recommended fields, and the page says Recommended layout: ….
  • A type you’ve already set up keeps your choices. Reset to recommended (click twice to confirm) replaces its Create and Edit / View screens with the recommended fields; your custom fields stay as they are.
  • New fields Worklynq adds later arrive switched off, so your forms don’t change on their own.

How tickets move: workflow types

Each ticket type has its own stages, and one of three workflow types decides how a ticket can move between them. Your System Admin sets this in Admin console → Work Configuration → Ticket Types.

Workflow typeHow tickets moveGood for
LinearOne stage up or down at a time, without skipping.Checklists, repairs, supplier orders
Free flowFrom any stage to any stage.Simple tasks, guest feedback
CustomYou choose where each stage can go next, so the workflow can split into branches and loop back.Reviews, approvals, anything with a “send it back” step

Every workflow has a starting stage, up to five stages in the middle, and up to four end stages. A ticket counts as resolved once it reaches an end stage — that’s what Unresolved and “open” mean in filters and Ask AI. Each end stage is marked as a success, a failure, or cancelled — so a request can end Approved, Rejected, or Withdrawn, and Home and reports know which way it finished.

Sections on a ticket

Besides fields, a ticket can show extra panels: the Time recorder for logging time, and, once those apps are connected, GitHub (branches, commits and pull requests) and the Claude and Cursor coding agents. The Sections column on Ticket Types shows which panels each type has. Click it to switch them on or off; apps that aren’t connected yet are greyed out, with a link to connect them.

Build a branching workflow

For example, a Menu Change goes from Proposed to Tasting; after tasting it can be sent back with Changes requested, move on to Costed, or be Rejected.

  1. Open Admin console → Work Configuration → Ticket Types and edit the type.
  2. Set Workflow type to Custom. It starts from the type’s current one-step-at-a-time moves.
  3. Set up the stages as usual: the start, the middle stages, and the end stages.
  4. In Branches, each stage lists the other stages as chips. Select the stages a ticket may move to next. The diagram above shows the result — moves forward as solid lines, moves back as dashed lines.
  5. Save. Worklynq won’t save a workflow with a stage that no path reaches from the start, or a working stage with nowhere to go next. End stages can have no moves out, or some to allow reopening.

Where the rules apply. On the board, dragging a card to a stage the workflow doesn’t allow shows This workflow doesn’t go from X to Y. On a ticket, the status menu greys out stages that aren’t a next step. Stage automations aren’t limited by the branches, and Linear and Free flow types work exactly as before.

Want a quick start? Reset to linear puts back the one-step-at-a-time moves, and you can add branches from there.
