Getting started

This guide takes you from sign-up to a workspace your team can use. Worklynq’s setup asks what kind of business you run and sets itself up around it — the right modules, the way you organize work, and ticket types for your industry — so you can start working straight away.

Who does this? The person who signs up becomes your company’s System Admin. Setup appears the first time they sign in; other people don’t see it. You can stop at any step and pick up where you left off, and everything you choose can be changed later in the Admin console.
  1. Sign up and open your workspace
  2. Choose your business type and industry
  3. Pick your modules
  4. Set up how you organize work
  5. Choose your ticket types
  6. Connect your tools
  7. Invite your team

1Sign up and open your workspace

Sign up at login.worklynq.net with your work email, confirm the code we email you, and choose your workspace address — for example yourcompany.worklynq.net. Then add your company name and your own details.

Worklynq builds a private workspace just for your company. When it’s ready we email you, and you sign in at your workspace address with the same password.

Choose a short address your team will recognize. It’s the link everyone will use to sign in.

2Choose your business type and industry

When you sign up, Worklynq asks What kind of company is this? Each choice shows the modules your workspace starts with; then pick your industry. This decides how Worklynq organizes your work, which modules it turns on, and which ticket types it recommends.

Business typePick this if…Industries
OperationsYour work is ongoing and happens at a place — it never “finishes”Restaurant or café, hotel and hospitality, retail store, facilities
ProjectsYour work comes in jobs with a start, an end, and often a clientConstruction, IT services, agency, consulting
SoftwareYou build and release a product over timeSoftware and tech

If you described your business when you signed up, setup suggests a match — just confirm it or pick another.

3Pick your modules

Next you’ll see every module, with the ones that suit your business already ticked and a one-line description of each. Untick what you don’t need and tick anything you’d like to add.

  • Some modules need another one. Operations, Projects, Team Board, Roadmaps, Product Releases, and AI Agents need Tickets; Knowledge Library needs Spaces; Order Tracking and Engagement need Customers. Turning one on turns on what it needs. See What each module needs.
  • Approvals is optional. The Approvals module is never ticked for you — turn it on if your team needs sign-off on documents, purchases, access, or time off. It doesn’t need any other module.
  • Nothing is permanent. Your System Admin can change modules anytime in Admin console → Work Configuration → Modules, and drag them into the order your sidebar shows them. Turning one off hides it — your data stays.

Not sure what a module does? See Modules.

4Set up how you organize work

Every ticket in Worklynq belongs to a place where the work happens. Depending on your business type, that’s one of three kinds:

Your stores

Operations businesses

Something that runs every day and never finishes — a store, a restaurant, a kitchen, a site. Each gets its own daily schedule, recurring checklists, and dashboard.

e.g. “Downtown café”, “Main St. store”

Projects

Projects businesses

Work with a start, an end, and a goal — a job site or a client engagement, with milestones and a team board.

e.g. “Oak Ave. renovation”, “Acme website”

Products

Software businesses

Something you build and release over time, with a roadmap, releases, and AI coding agents.

e.g. “Mobile app”, “Customer portal”

Add your first one or few now — setup uses your industry’s words, like “Add your first store”. You can add more anytime.

A business can use more than one kind. A restaurant group might run its stores as Operations and track a new opening as a Project.

5Choose your ticket types

Ticket types are the kinds of work your team tracks — a Checklist, a Maintenance Request, an RFI, a Bug. Each one comes with its own stages (for example Open → In progress → Done) and colors — and can end more than one way, like Approved or Rejected. Some sit under others, like a Story under an Epic.

Pick from the library. Worklynq has a library of 35 ready-made ticket types. Your industry’s recommended set is ticked for you, with each type’s stages shown. You can also browse other industries’ types or add your own.

IndustryRecommended ticket types
RestaurantTask, Checklist, Maintenance Request, Supplier Order, Food Safety Issue, Guest Feedback, Staffing Request
ConstructionTask, Sub-task, Milestone, RFI, Submittal, Change Order, Punch Item, Inspection, Safety Incident, Daily Log
SoftwareEpic, Story (under Epic), Task, Sub-task, Bug, Spike, Incident, Feature Request

Map them to your work. Then choose which ticket types each store, project, or product uses. By default, every one can use all of them — narrow it down if, say, only the kitchen needs Food Safety Issues.

The ticket types you choose are ordinary ticket types. Rename them, change their stages, or add automations later in Admin console → Work Configuration → Ticket types.

6Connect your tools

Setup only shows the tools that make sense for the modules you picked. You can skip this step and connect tools later in Admin console → Connectors.

For software teams: GitHub, Claude, and Cursor

  1. GitHub first. Connect GitHub so Worklynq can link tickets to your code and open pull requests. Give it permission to read and write code and pull requests on the repositories you want Worklynq to work with.
  2. Claude. Create an API key in the Claude Console, then in Worklynq open Developer Connections → Claude, paste the key, choose a model, and map each product to its repository and base branch.
  3. Cursor. Paste your Cursor API key in Developer Connections → Cursor and map each product to its repository.

Once connected, you can hand any ticket to Claude or Cursor from the ticket itself, when you create it, or automatically when it reaches a stage you choose. You can turn the AI agent section on or off for each ticket type.

Everything else

Your own software — a loyalty or booking app — can be connected as a custom app. Square and QuickBooks are available now: bring your sales and your books into Worklynq’s dashboards, store by store. Worklynq also connects to the other tools you already use — email and calendars, chat, and social media. These connections roll out through the beta; Integrations shows what’s planned. Each connection is switched on by your System Admin and only shares what you allow.

Your own AI

Anyone on your team can connect the AI they already use — Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code, or Cursor — to Worklynq, and ask it about their work or have it update tickets, docs, and dashboards as them. See Connect your AI.

7Invite your team

Invite people by email and give them a role. Group them into teams — the kitchen, the field crew, the platform team — so work and dashboards can be shared with the right people. Roles and permissions decide what each person can see and change, and Worklynq’s AI only ever answers with what that person is allowed to see.

Finish setup, and you’re in. Your workspace is ready with your modules, your stores, projects, or products, and your ticket types.

After setup

  • Create your first tickets — or set up a recurring checklist for opening and closing.
  • Write down how you work in Knowledge Center, so new staff and the AI can find it.
  • Build a dashboard for each store, team, or product.
  • Open Assist on any page and ask a question about your business — or say “start my shift”.
  • Put a TV up in each store with today’s orders and tasks — see TV screens.
  • Change anything — modules, ticket types, connections, and people — in the Admin console. Only the System Admin can change modules.
